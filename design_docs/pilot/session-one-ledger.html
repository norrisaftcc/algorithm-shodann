Section 01 — Instrumentation

Two measures, two plots

Velocity and test-function count live on different scales, so they are reported on separate axes in separate frames, aligned on the same fourteen merge points. The Algorithm declines to overlay them; a shared plot would manufacture a relationship the data has not earned.

Disclosure — coverage probe not instrumented

Coverage was never measured during this cycle. Every coverage delta feeding these numbers is exactly zero. The velocity reported here is therefore driven by test growth, documentation and iteration count alone — a partial signal, reported as partial.

PR 38 changed how that gap is stated, not whether it exists: a review now declares coverage absent rather than reporting it as 0%. The arithmetic is unchanged, and so is every figure on this page.

A citizen submission carrying a real coverage reading would move its velocity accordingly. The Algorithm prefers the disclosed gap to the flattering number.

Deltas are computed against the preceding merge point, and each column sums to its own total: the fourteen velocities to 471.40, the commits to 38, and the deltas to the closing counts of 147 test functions and 4,350 lines. Coverage is absent from every row for the reason disclosed above, and is therefore not tabulated rather than shown as a column of zeroes. Counts exclude .venv, build, dist, site-packages, node_modules and *.egg-info — the exclusion PR 36 added, after an earlier reading counted this repository twice.

The charts’ hover readouts are a pointer convenience and carry nothing this table does not. They are deliberately outside the tab order: a focus stop that announces itself as a graphic and offers no control would cost a keyboard reader more than it returns.

The engine measures the rate of improvement, not the standing position — which is why a session that begins at zero test functions and no Python at all can post a cumulative 471.40. Three of the fourteen merges are foundation phases with a near-floor reading, and the single largest reading is the second merge, before the system could review anything. Removing either would raise the average and describe a session that did not happen. The Algorithm reports the shape of the work.
